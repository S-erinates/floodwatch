RADAR PHYSICS

📡 How Does Radar See Water?

Unlike optical satellites blinded by cloud cover or night, Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) transmits active L-band microwave pulses (~24 cm wavelength). Calm water surfaces act as a mirror (specular reflection), deflecting radar energy away from the sensor so water pixels appear extremely dark (low dB). HH polarization captures surface roughness variations, while HV cross-polarization is sensitive to volume scattering in vegetation, helping separate open water from flooded forests and agricultural wetlands.

REGIONAL CONTEXT & PIPELINE

🌍 Study Area & Methodology

Study Area: Inner Niger Delta, Mali — one of Africa's largest seasonal floodplains. Two co-registered NISAR GCOV scenes (L-band, track 138, frame 081, descending orbit), acquired on 17 July 2026 (pre-flood baseline) and 21 October 2026 (flood season), were retrieved via ASF Vertex.

Processing Pipeline: Using Python (h5py), the HHHH and HVHV covariance grids under science/LSAR/GCOV/grids/frequencyA/ were block-averaged from 20 m native pixels to 60 m in linear power, converted to decibels (10 * log10), and classified as open water where HH < -19 dB and HV < -24 dB. A 5 × 5 majority filter then removed isolated noisy pixels. An area of about 1,525 km² affected by artificial radar brightening in the 21 October HV image was excluded, so all areas (km²) and the net change refer only to the valid analysis area. As an independent check, the change in HH backscatter (> 3 dB drop or rise) was compared with the classified water gain and loss.

INTERPRETATION & ROADMAP

📊 Hydrological Findings, Limitations & Future Impact

Hydrological Drivers

The Inner Niger Delta floods every year when rainy-season runoff from the Guinean highlands (July–September) travels down the Niger and Bani rivers, overtopping their banks and filling the floodplain, with peak extent typically in October–November. The roughly threefold increase in open water between July and October is consistent with this annual flood pulse.

Scientific Limitations

Fixed-threshold binary classification across two dates can be influenced by wind-induced capillary waves (which roughen water and raise backscatter) and by double-bounce scattering in flooded vegetation, which makes partly submerged areas appear bright and can lead to underestimating flooded area.

NISAR Future Applications

With NISAR's systematic 12-day repeat cycle, FloodWatch could be extended to longer multi-year flood-pulse time series, compared with rainfall and river-level records, and developed toward automatic alerts for unusual changes in water coverage.
